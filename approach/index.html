Our 21-day decision sprint.

A fixed 21-day system that turns early-stage uncertainty into a defensible concept direction.

Most innovation projects fail not because of execution, but because the foundation underneath was never made explicit. Anté's methodology is structured to surface that foundation phase by phase, with the client involved at every checkpoint.

§ 02 · The methodology Five phases · one decision

Five phases, one decision.

Every phase builds on the last. Every output is traceable back to real evidence. The client is present at each checkpoint — not as a spectator, but as the decision-maker with full context.

Phase 01 — Context & Evidence

Context & Evidence

We start by understanding the reality behind the brief, not the assumptions around it. Through structured stakeholder interviews and targeted market input, we map what is actually known, what is assumed and what is missing.

This step is critical because most innovations fail before they are even made, in the form of untested assumptions that quietly shape direction.

→ output: structured evidence base, separating assumptions from validated input
Phase 02 — Problem Definition

Problem Definition

Once the evidence is clear, we define the real problem to be solved. Not the initial request. Not the internal narrative. But the underlying challenge that explains why the decision exists in the first place.

We translate fragmented input into a single structured problem definition, including the criteria that will later determine what "good" looks like.

→ output: defined problem statement + weighted decision criteria
Phase 03 — Concept Exploration

Concept Exploration

From a single well-defined problem, we deliberately explore multiple possible futures. We develop 3 fundamentally different concept directions, not variations, but strategic alternatives.

Each direction represents a different way of solving the same problem, making the trade-offs clear instead of leaving them implicit. This is the point where most teams choose too quickly. We don't.

→ output: three distinct and comparable concept directions
Phase 04 — Decision Matrix

Decision Matrix

We evaluate all directions using a structured, evidence-based comparison model. Every trade-off matrix is linked back to insights from each previous phase. This removes opinion from the decision.

What remains is traceable reasoning, not preference, but justification. The goal is a concept direction that can be defended internally and externally.

→ output: validated concept decision with full trade-off transparency
Phase 05 — Implementation Brief

Implementation Brief

We translate the chosen direction into a structured brief that engineering or external partners can act on immediately. This includes scope, requirements, open questions and design constraints, all derived from the decision process, based on facts.

The result is clarity at execution level without losing strategic intent.

→ output: build-ready implementation brief
