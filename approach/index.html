§ 01 · The methodology Seven phases · 1 decision

Seven phases, one decision.

Most innovation projects fail not because of execution, but because the foundation underneath was never made explicit. Anté's methodology is structured to surface that foundation phase by phase, with the client involved at every checkpoint.

1
Phase 1

Preparation

Before the sprint begins, we map the project context. Who the stakeholders are. What's been tried. What's already known. What scope of decision is on the table. The output is a structured project profile your team can sign off on before any interview happens.

What you receive

A project briefing document and a calendar with confirmed interview slots.

2
Phase 2

Analysis

Eight stakeholder interviews. Internal team, external customers, technical experts, market voices. Every conversation is recorded, transcribed, and analysed individually, then synthesised across interviews to surface shared themes, contradictions, and pressure points. Desk research runs in parallel: market, competition, technology, regulation. All evidence enters the living analysis map.

What you receive

A synthesis dossier, the first version of the analysis map, and a stakeholder alignment matrix.

3
Phase 3

Definition

A structured workshop with your core team. We open with what we call the Insight Reveal: the moment where the patterns and contradictions from the analysis are made visible to the people who lived inside the situation. From there, the workshop narrows toward a sharp problem definition, the underlying sub-problems, and the criteria by which we will later evaluate concept directions. Critically: criteria weighting happens here, before any concept exists. Your priorities cannot be reverse-engineered to match a preferred outcome.

What you receive

A defined problem statement, a structured set of sub-problems, and a weighted criteria framework.

4
Phase 4

Exploration

A creative workshop. Three to five concept directions emerge, fundamentally different approaches to the same problem, each linked back to specific sub-problems. After the workshop we run a structured checkpoint: do the criteria still hold, are there new sub-problems, has anything shifted in the analysis map? Then we proceed.

What you receive

A clustered set of concept directions with explicit links to the sub-problems they address.

5
Phase 5

Concept Development

We narrow to three concepts and develop each one fully, visually, technically, and operationally. Depending on the project, this means product renders, service blueprints, or wireframes. Per concept, per criterion, we build an evidence dossier: every claim about feasibility or impact is traced back to interview quotes, research sources, or workshop input. Halfway through this phase we run a Concept Preview with your stakeholders: early, qualitative feedback that enters the analysis as an additional layer.

What you receive

Three fully visualised concepts and an evidence dossier per concept.

6
Phase 6

Decision

The trade-off. Not a scorecard but a layered evidence comparison. Every score is sourced. Every claim is auditable. We present the trade-off live with your team, including the option to shift criteria weighting in the moment to test how robust the recommendation is. The output is a defended choice, not an opinion.

What you receive

A complete trade-off matrix, the recommended concept with full justification, and a risk profile per direction.

7
Phase 7

Handover

The conclusion is two documents, not one. The Decision Brief, five pages, written for the person signing off, covering why this concept, what the strategic context is, what evidence supports the choice. The Implementation Spec, as detailed as needed, written for whoever builds: specifications, requirements, scope boundaries, acceptance criteria. Plus the final analysis map, fully traceable from first interview to final decision.

What you receive

Two handover documents and the complete project archive.

A note on numbering: this is structured as seven phases because Preparation is genuinely a phase. If you prefer "six phases" as the public number to match the project plan v2, Preparation merges into the project intake and the methodology starts at Analysis. Either works. The work is identical.

§ 02 · The living analysis map Built in Kumu · auditable

One map, growing through every phase.

[ Kumu screenshot · element types & connections ]

Most consultancies hand over a deck at the end. Anté's primary deliverable is something different: a living map that grows phase by phase, where every element traces back to a specific source and every connection has visible evidence behind it.

At the start of the engagement the map holds your stakeholders. After Phase 2 it includes pain points, opportunities, and leverage points found in the interviews. After Phase 3, the sub-problems and criteria are added. After Phase 5, the concepts and their evidence layers. After Phase 6, the final decision sits on the map with every supporting source one click away.

The map is the reason our trade-off can be defended. Not because we say so. Because every score in the final matrix points back to a specific quote, a specific source, or a specific workshop moment. Auditable evidence, not opinion.

§ 03 · Three concept archetypes Same methodology · adapted output

One methodology, three concept paths.

A concept can be a physical product, a service, or a digital system. The methodology stays identical across all three. The visualisation language adapts.

01 · Archetype

Physical product

When the concept is a product (hardware, equipment, an industrial component), we develop it through high-fidelity renders, materials specification, and production-feasibility analysis. The Implementation Spec includes technical drawings, materials list, and tolerances for downstream engineering.

02 · Archetype

Service or experience

When the concept is a service (a customer journey, a clinical workflow, an operational process), we develop it as a service blueprint, with touchpoints, customer journey maps, and process flows. The Implementation Spec includes service specifications, training material, and KPI frameworks.

03 · Archetype

Digital system

When the concept is software, a platform, or a digital tool, we develop it as wireframes, user flows, and information architecture. The Implementation Spec includes functional requirements, user stories, and architectural recommendations for downstream development teams.

§ 04 · Boundaries Four declarative limits

What we don't do.

Our methodology has explicit boundaries. They exist because they protect the value of what we deliver.

01 We don't build the concept we recommend. Engineering and development is your next partner, not us.
02 We don't introduce you to "preferred" development partners. We have no financial relationship with any agency, engineering firm, or contractor.
03 We don't take ongoing retainers. Each engagement is fixed-scope, fixed-price, fixed-time.
04 We don't make recommendations our methodology can't substantiate. If the evidence isn't there, we'll tell you the evidence isn't there.
