AI Product Definition · Fictional example

PackSight

A camera unit that checks every pack on the line, learns a customer's own defects from a few examples, and shows the operator what it found and why. Sold as a new product next to the machines.

For whom

  • User: the line operator and the quality lead at the customer.
  • Second user: the builder's service technician, who installs and tunes the unit.
  • Buyer: the plant manager at the customer.

The problem

Customers check packs by eye, or with fixed-rule cameras that need an expert for every new product. Defects slip through, good packs get rejected, and every changeover costs time.

What the product does

  • Checks every pack as it passes the camera.
  • Learns a new defect from about ten marked examples, without a programmer.
  • Shows each flagged pack with the reason highlighted, so the operator can accept or reject.

Role of AI

  • Does: recognises defects in images and learns new ones from examples. The product does not exist without it.
  • Does not: stop the line, change machine settings or decide what counts as a defect. The operator confirms.

Features, by priority

Must

Five common defects out of the box. Teach a new defect from examples. Flagged pack with the reason shown.

Should

Report per shift. “This flag was wrong” feedback.

Later

Automatic rejection through the line controller. Remote support.

Data it relies on

Images from the unit's own camera. The customer's examples of good and bad packs. No images of people. Everything stays on the customer's site.

Risks, and how we handle them

  • Missed defect: the unit supports the operator and does not replace the final check.
  • Lighting or packaging changes: a setup guide and a test card.
  • Customers wary of sharing images: nothing leaves the site.

Success criteria

  • A new defect is taught without calling service.
  • Operators act on the flags instead of ignoring them.
  • Fewer good packs rejected than with fixed-rule cameras.

Open questions

  • Sold as a unit or as a subscription?
  • Who owns the images and the trained model?
  • Which partner builds the model and the hardware?

Where the team disagreed, and what we settled on

  • A chatbot (sales) became a vision unit: the customer's problem is quality, not conversation.
  • A premium feature (management) and fewer visits (service): one product that sells on its own and that the customer trains on site.
  • Line stops (engineering): in version 1 the unit advises and never controls the line.

Brief for the builder

Version 1: one camera unit for flat and tray packs, five defects out of the box, new defects taught on a tablet, no line control. Out of scope: automatic rejection, other pack types, remote support.
