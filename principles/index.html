How we look at AI products.

Eight principles we use to judge AI in a product, ours or anyone's. Each one comes with a red flag: a sign that a feature is not ready to be built. Use them on your own idea.

01

Start from the decision the user has to make, not from the model.

Why it matters

People don't buy a model. They want to decide or act faster. A feature built around what the model can do often answers a question nobody asked.

Red flag

The first slide of the plan is about the model, not about the user.

Source: Google, People + AI Guidebook

02

If the AI is wrong, what does it cost the user? Design for that moment.

Why it matters

Every AI system is sometimes wrong. The cost of a mistake decides how much the user has to check, and how clearly the product should show its uncertainty.

Red flag

Nobody can tell you what the screen shows when the AI gets it wrong.

Source: Microsoft, HAX Guidelines 2 and 9: "Make clear how well the system can do what it can do", "Support efficient correction"

03

AI should remove a step, not add a screen.

Why it matters

The value is in work the user no longer has to do. An AI tab that people have to remember to open rarely gets opened.

Red flag

The AI lives in its own tab or chat window, apart from the work.

Source: Microsoft, HAX Guideline 4: "Show contextually relevant information"

04

Your data decides more than your model.

Why it matters

Everyone has access to the same models. What makes your product better is the data you have, how clean it is, and whether you may use it.

Red flag

Nobody has checked whether the data exists, is usable and may be used.

Source: Google, People + AI Guidebook

05

A feature nobody asked for is a cost, not a differentiator.

Why it matters

Every AI feature has to be built, explained, maintained and paid for each time it runs. If users don't need it, it only adds cost and confusion.

Red flag

The main reason for the feature is that a competitor has one.

06

Keep a human exit wherever the AI can fail.

Why it matters

People trust a system more when they can step in: correct it, ignore it or reach a person. Without an exit, one bad answer can cost you the user.

Red flag

The only way out of a wrong answer is to start over.

Source: Microsoft, HAX Guidelines 8 and 9: "Support efficient dismissal", "Support efficient correction"

07

Define “good enough” before you build, and test the behaviour, not the demo.

Why it matters

A demo shows the best case. Real users bring messy input. Agree upfront what quality is enough, and test with real cases.

Red flag

Success is judged on a demo with hand-picked examples.

Source: Google, People + AI Guidebook

08

Every AI call has a running cost, and the model will change under you. Plan for both.

Why it matters

Unlike most software, AI costs money every time it is used. Providers also update and retire models. Both affect your margin and how your product behaves.

Red flag

The business case has no line for usage costs or model updates.

Principles without a source are our own. The guidelines we refer to are published by Google PAIR and Microsoft HAX.
